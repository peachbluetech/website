Claude is genuinely useful for media buying now, but how useful depends entirely on one question: can it see your data? Without access to your accounts, Claude is a smart intern with amnesia. With access, it becomes something closer to an analyst who has read every row of your ad history. This guide covers both modes honestly, and how to set up the second one.

Disclosure: I am the founder of Peachblue, which builds one of the tools below. The workflow advice stands regardless of whose tool you use.

## What Claude does well with no setup at all

Plenty, actually. Paste in your creative copy and Claude critiques hooks and angles competently. Paste a screenshot of a results table and it reads the numbers. Describe your product and audience and it drafts briefs, generates angle lists, and rewrites headlines in volume. For creative ideation, it is already better than most brainstorm meetings.

The ceiling is just as real:

- **It cannot see your accounts.** Every number arrives by copy-paste, which means stale snapshots, tiny samples, and no follow-up questions the data could answer.
- **Manual data entry invites errors.** A pasted table loses the context around it: attribution window, date range, currency, which campaigns were excluded.
- **It will reason confidently about numbers it half-remembers.** Ask a follow-up two screens later and the model may be working from a garbled version of what you pasted.

Copy-paste Claude is a writing tool. The unlock is making it an analysis tool.

## The unlock: MCP

The Model Context Protocol is an open standard that lets Claude call tools that fetch live data. Instead of you pasting numbers in, Claude queries the source and reasons over what comes back. The ads ecosystem is adopting it fast: Meta now ships its own ads MCP server, generic data connectors expose raw platform metrics, and analytics tools are exposing their processed data.

Those options are not interchangeable, and the difference decides what Claude can actually do for you.

## Meta's MCP vs an intelligence layer: what changes

Connecting Meta's own MCP (or a generic connector) gives Claude the same rows you see in Ads Manager: one platform, per-ad metrics, no interpretation. That is genuinely useful for quick lookups. But every hard question a media buyer asks lives above the row level, and raw rows leave Claude to reconstruct that level from scratch inside the chat, every single time, with limited context and no memory of how it did it yesterday.

Peachblue's MCP sits on top of synced, analyzed data instead, and the practical differences stack up fast:

| You ask Claude | With Meta's MCP alone | With Peachblue's MCP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "What is my best creative?" | Best *ad* per metric. The same creative split across 12 ads looks like 12 mediocre ads | Creatives grouped by fingerprint across every ad and platform, ranked by composite score |
| "Which hook styles are winning?" | No such concept exists in the data | Every creative is tagged across 31 dimensions; hook style is a filter, not a research project |
| "Compare Meta vs TikTok performance" | Cannot; one platform per connection | One connection covers Meta, TikTok, Google Ads, and Amazon DSP |
| "Rank last month and tell me what to scale" | Claude invents a ranking method per conversation; answers drift between chats | Same composite score the app uses; Claude's answer matches your team's dashboard |
| "What do my winners have in common?" | Claude would need to inspect every creative itself, which does not scale in chat | Pattern detection and archetypes are precomputed; Claude retrieves and reasons |
| "What is Reddit saying about my brand?" | Not ad-platform data at all | Brand Intel mentions, sentiment, and the editorial brief are tools Claude can call |

Two quieter advantages compound over time. **History**: Peachblue keeps what it syncs, so analysis is not capped by platform retention limits (Amazon DSP, for instance, only retains about 60 days of report data; your Peachblue history builds forward past it). **Context efficiency**: tools return ranked, aggregated payloads instead of raw row dumps, so Claude spends its context reasoning rather than arithmetic, which in practice means fewer wrong numbers.

To be fair to the raw option: Meta's MCP is the right tool for platform-native lookups, and nothing stops you from connecting both. The distinction is what you want Claude to be. Raw rows make it a query runner. An intelligence layer makes it an analyst.

Peachblue's server exposes the same 17 tools that power our in-app agent, over your Meta, TikTok, Google Ads, and Amazon DSP data. Everything is read-only; nothing can touch your campaigns.

## Setting it up

Prerequisites: a Peachblue account (Pro or higher) with at least one platform synced, and Claude Desktop or Claude Code.

1. In Peachblue, open Settings and go to the MCP tab. Copy your connection URL.
2. In Claude Desktop: Settings, then Connectors, then Add custom connector. Paste the URL.
3. Approve the OAuth sign-in with your Peachblue account when prompted. No API keys.

Full setup details, including Claude Code, are in the [MCP docs](/docs/mcp), and the complete tool list is in the [tool reference](/docs/mcp-tools).

## What to actually ask

The queries that earn their keep daily:

- "How did last week compare to the week before, and what drove the change?"
- "Rank my top 10 creatives by composite score this month and tell me what they have in common."
- "Compare my two hero videos head to head. Which one deserves the next budget increase?"
- "Which hook styles are winning in my account right now?"
- "Which headlines are underperforming across my Google RSAs?"
- "What is Reddit saying about my brand this week, and is there an angle worth testing?"

Then the compound move: because Claude holds the whole conversation, you can chain analysis into action. Pull the patterns behind your winners, then in the same thread have Claude draft five new concepts that fit those patterns. Analysis to brief in one sitting, grounded in your own data at every step.

## What this replaces, and what it does not

It replaces the export-paste-reformat loop, the "quick question" that used to mean opening four dashboard tabs, and a decent share of weekly report assembly. It does not replace your analytics app for visual browsing, it does not buy or edit media (by design), and it does not replace judgment: Claude with your data is a sharper analyst, not a decision-maker.

Honest limitations: you need a Claude plan that supports connectors, MCP access starts on Peachblue's Pro tier ([pricing](/pricing)), and agency workspaces with per-client scoping are coming soon. Fair-use rate limits apply but normal analytical use never meets them.
